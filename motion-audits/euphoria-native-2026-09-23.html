euphoria-native: the streak moment moves, but it doesn’t perform

A gamified finance-learning app for teens, benchmarked against Duolingo. The audit covers the earned streak moment (StreakIgnite), the shared week tracker (WeekStreak), and the Home Today card that hosts both.

Mobile app · Expo 57 · React Native 0.86 · Reanimated 4.5 · Skia 2.6 · fast-confetti 2 · number-flow
12Findings 1Critical 5Important 6Opportunities
OVERALL

The structure is right: one earned moment, one clock, a reduced-motion path, and confetti kept to the moments that matter. The execution reads as generated because every beat runs at constant speed and everything scales from the same centre point. There is no anticipation you can feel, no follow-through, and no character. The flame is a stroked icon that grows and shrinks, the glow is a flat disc, and Atlas, the one thing Duolingo proves carries the emotion, only shows up at 24pt in the tracker. Fix the timeline's easing first; everything else rests on it.

Issues to address
CriticalJakubJhey

The whole moment runs on a piecewise-linear timeline

Whatt animates 0→1 with Easing.linear, and every style maps t through interpolate() between keyframes, which is also linear. Each beat moves at constant speed and changes speed abruptly at every stage boundary.

Why it mattersThis is the single biggest "robotic" tell. Linear motion with velocity jumps has no weight, so anticipation doesn't read as wind-up and the burst doesn't read as release. Easing is "the most important part of any animation."

Recommended motionKeep the one clock, but ease inside each beat: split t into per-stage local progress and pass each through its own curve. Build: ease-in (gathering). Burn/burst: ease-out-back (release with overshoot). Resolve: a critically damped settle. Or drive the scale with withSequence of eased withTiming and withSpring steps.

A full-screen white frame on a dark UI

WhatA pure #FFFFFF layer covering the whole screen fades to 0.92 opacity for about 256ms.

Why it mattersOn a near-black app it's a hard luma slam that reads as a glitch, and it's uncomfortable for light-sensitive users. It's a single flash, so it's under the WCAG three-flash threshold, but it isn't polish. Duolingo's spike happens on a light UI.

Recommended motionReplace it with a local bloom: a radial gradient centred on the flame that scales 0.6→1.25 while fading out, peaking around 70% brightness at the centre. The payoff stays where the eye already is.

The glow is a flat, solid disc

WhatThe glow is a 160pt View with a solid Colors.dark.streak background, animated with opacity and scale.

Why it mattersLight has falloff and a hard-edged disc doesn't, so it reads as a sticker. In the recorded frames it was the most "cheap" element on screen.

Recommended motionRender it with Skia (Circle + RadialGradient): streak colour at about 60% in the centre, 20% at 55%, transparent at the edge. Keep the same scale curve.

Jhey Tompkins — Experimentation & Delight

Secondary

Concern. A rare, earned moment with no character in it.

What's working well

Issues to address
ImportantJheyJakub

The flame only scales uniformly from its centre

WhatA stroked outline icon, UIcon "flame" at strokeWidth 1.6, runs through a uniform scale curve. There's no squash and stretch, no arc, no colour change, and no secondary motion.

Why it mattersUniform scale is the most generic motion there is. Duolingo's flame changes from desaturated grey to vivid and bursts; ours is the same orange line throughout.

Recommended motionA filled flame, pinned at the bottom (transformOrigin: bottom). Build: squash to 1.14×0.80 (anticipation). Burst: stretch to 0.86×1.22 and rise 10pt. Then overshoot 1.06×0.95 and settle. Colour goes from a muted grey to streak across the burn.

ImportantJhey

Atlas pops in from scale 0

WhatZoomIn starts at scale 0, so Atlas grows out of a point inside the circle.

Why it mattersGrowing from 0 reads as "appearing from nothing". Starting at about 0.6 with opacity reads as "arriving", and the overshoot has something to land on.

Recommended motionA custom entering animation: scale 0.6→1 with opacity 0→1 on the existing spring (ζ≈0.56, about 12% overshoot).

ImportantJheyJakub

The number roll runs on its own clock

WhatNumberFlow uses its library-default roll timing, triggered by a setTimeout at FLASH_END, while the count's scale bounce is on the master t.

Why it mattersTwo clocks can drift apart, for example when the JS thread is busy after a lesson. Then the digit lands after the bounce has finished and the beat splits in two.

Recommended motionPass explicit roll timing so the digit lands inside RESOLVE (about 400ms, ease-out-quint, a small overshoot to −4pt, then settle), and time the scale bounce to the digit's landing.

Opportunities
  • Atlas celebration twirl (your pick): crouch as anticipation (squash 1.12×0.85, ~180ms), launch and spin 360° on a small upward arc with stretch (0.9×1.12, ~420ms), land with overshoot, then settle into a proud pose (~300ms). About 900ms total, placed on the BURST beat. Build it in code now; your Blender Atlas replaces the sprite later.
  • Duolingo's phoenix idea: at milestones (7 / 30 / 100 days) Atlas visibly changes form. Duolingo's blog credits this character transformation with making milestones land.
  • Confetti with a brand shape: swap the square flakes for small star or coin shapes drawn from an SVG texture (fast-confetti supports this). streak-ignite.tsx:192
  • Sound: a short CC0 chime from Kenney at the burst via expo-audio. This needs a native rebuild.

Through Jhey's lens: the streak is where Euphoria is allowed to be loud, so spend it on Atlas, the one thing that's actually yours.
